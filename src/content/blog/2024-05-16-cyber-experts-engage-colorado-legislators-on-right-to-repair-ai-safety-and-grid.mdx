## Bridging the Gap: Cybersecurity Professionals Engage at Colorado's State Capitol

Recently, Colorado hosted its inaugural "Hacker on the Hill" event, an initiative spearheaded by Andrew Brandt and the "Elect More Hackers" organization. This unique gathering brought together approximately 30 cybersecurity experts at the Colorado State Capitol, facilitating direct engagement with state legislators on critical technology and policy issues during their active legislative session. The goal was to ensure that policymakers have access to expert perspectives as they deliberate on laws impacting the digital landscape.

The discussions spanned a range of pertinent topics, reflecting the complex interplay between technology, security, and public policy. Key areas included AI safety and security, the infrastructure considerations of data center build-outs, and the imperative for grid modernization.

A significant point of discussion revolved around Colorado's "Right to Repair" legislation. This important consumer protection measure, enacted last year through HB23-1011, allows individuals and independent repair shops to access the necessary parts, tools, and information to repair their own electronic devices and equipment. Legislators sought direct input from the cybersecurity delegation regarding ongoing lobbying efforts to introduce exceptions to this law, particularly those citing national security concerns related to critical security systems. The cybersecurity professionals provided their insights and guidance on the potential ramifications of such exemptions, emphasizing the delicate balance between security imperatives and the right to maintain ownership and control over technology.

Another pressing topic was AI safety, with particular emphasis on its potential impacts on minors and mental health. This area highlighted the proactive stance required from policymakers to address the societal implications of rapidly advancing AI technologies. The delegation engaged in thoughtful conversations about responsible AI development and deployment, stressing the need for safeguards to protect vulnerable populations.

Additionally, participants discussed a bill focused on grid resiliency and modernization. As critical infrastructure becomes increasingly digitized and interconnected, ensuring the security and robustness of the power grid is paramount. These discussions provided legislators with expert perspectives on the technical challenges and opportunities in fortifying essential services against evolving threats.

This event offered invaluable insight into the legislative process, the priorities of state representatives, and the nuanced dynamics of state versus federal legislative action. It underscored how direct engagement with technical experts can inform and refine policy-making. Beyond the policy discussions, it served as an excellent networking opportunity, connecting cybersecurity leaders from diverse backgrounds—including consultants, CISOs, engineers, and AI specialists—to foster a collaborative approach to addressing shared challenges.

Such direct engagement between the tech community and policymakers is crucial for crafting informed and effective legislation in our increasingly digital world. It is a powerful model for ensuring that technology policy is grounded in practical realities and expert understanding, ultimately leading to stronger, more resilient outcomes for citizens and critical infrastructure alike.
